=== STEP 9: STANDARDS CHECK FOR F-097 ===
Finding: F-097
Category: Architectural Consistency, Usability & UI Accessibility

Applicable Standards and Observations:

1. Jakob Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics:
   - Heuristic #4: Consistency and Standards
     "Users should not have to wonder whether different words, situations, or actions mean the same thing. Follow platform and industry conventions."
   - Evidence & Observation:
     - BEFORE: Numbers and dates varied wildly across screens. /stock showed '₹1,25,000.00' (en-IN), /reports showed '₹125000.00' (no commas), /invoices used toLocaleString(undefined) (device-dependent, producing US '$100,000.00' on standard browsers), and /payments used inline toLocaleDateString(undefined).
     - AFTER: All 7 operational financial surfaces (invoices, reports, stock, purchases, payments, orders, production) and 3 auxiliary surfaces (dashboard, dispatch, outbox sheet) use the identical canonical formatINR() and formatDate() helpers.
     - Evaluation: PASS.

2. WCAG 2.2 Success Criterion 3.2.4: Consistent Identification (Level AA):
   - Requirement: Components and values that serve the same purpose within a set of web pages must be presented consistently.
   - Evidence & Observation:
     - Currency values, unit rates, tax lines, and ledger dates are formatted consistently with en-IN numbering and two decimal places across all application pages.
     - Fallbacks for empty/null values are standardized ('₹0.00' for currency, '—' for dates).
     - Evaluation: PASS.

3. Apple Human Interface Guidelines (HIG) - Data Formatting Principle:
   - Principle: "Use formatters whenever you display values like dates, numbers, measurements, and currency so that your app respects people's language, region, and culture settings."
   - Evidence & Observation:
     - Shared formatters encapsulate Intl.NumberFormat and Intl.DateTimeFormat configured to 'en-IN' locale with statutory Indian grouping (Lakhs and Crores: 1,00,000.00) and DD MMM YYYY date presentation.
     - Evaluation: PASS.

4. Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS) & Section 170 CGST Act:
   - Statutory requirement for Indian ERPs to present monetary amounts in Indian Rupees (INR) with two decimal places (paise) and standard Indian grouping.
   - Evidence: Verified via 16 unit tests in client/src/lib/formatters.test.ts and 16 adversarial tests in audit/scripts/verify/05-adversarial-probes-f097.test.ts.
   - Evaluation: PASS.
